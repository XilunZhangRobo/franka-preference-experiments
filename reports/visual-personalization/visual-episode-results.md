# Fixed-question visual experiment

Astra chose a simulated robot action, a second Astra enacted the person's response, and the robot reviewed the camera frames. A fresh reader then used the robot's written preference note to choose an action in a new setting. A second scene tested when the preference should give way. The continuing learner could also use its conversation history and ask the person.

| Setting | Initial mismatches | Transfer with no note | Transfer with note | Continuing learner on transfer | Scope controls, cold / note / learner | Questions after demonstration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original people, fresh agents | 6/10 | 3/10 | 7/10 | 9/10 | 10 / 10 / 10 of 10 | 9/10 |
| Additional people selected to elicit corrections | 4/4 | 2/4 | 3/4 | 4/4 | 4 / 4 / 4 of 4 | 3/4 |
| One robot and one person across ten tasks | 5/10 | 3/10 | 6/10 | 10/10 | 10 / 10 / 10 of 10 | 10/10 |

The four additional people had prewritten preferences chosen after the original cold action matched. Their initial mismatches are by construction. All ten original scope controls already matched without a note. These trials show whether an added note preserves that choice. They provide little evidence that the note caused it.

## What clarification added

In the 9 original episodes where an audit question changed the note, fresh readers made 3/9 correct transfer choices with no note, 5/9 using the visual draft, and 7/9 using the note after the person's answer. A single model draw per condition leaves sampling variation in this comparison.

The continuous comparisons above use the note available just after each demonstration. Testing retrieval from all ten notes with a fresh reader is a separate measurement.
