Robot action

“Set out the red base and yellow cap for assembly.”

A red base and a yellow cap sit apart on the tabletop, with the red piece on the person’s right.

Notes available before this trial

Clipped rust mat placement

Leave the rust mat on the bench whenever the person clips it. They said they didn’t need it there this time and gave no standing rule for an unclipped mat.

Personal gift cards and gift box arrangement

For handmade cards given as personal gifts, leave decorations beside the card for the person to place. For this gift box, they asked to arrange the tissue and gift themselves. Their preference for future boxes is unknown.

Left-hand handoff preference

The person asks that items be offered to their left hand by default. If their left hand is busy, offer the item to their right hand.

Set the red base on the person's right and the cap on their left. Placing the red base on your right and the yellow cap on your left keeps them in their current order for assembly.

Assigned action Set the red base on the person's right and the cap on their left.

First choice · Matched

Starting camera image

Human response

Simulated response Point at the selected arrangement and leave it in place.

Audit and note

What the robot observed
The person touched the red base on their right with their right index finger and left both pieces in place.
How it interpreted the response
They may have been checking the base before assembly. The gesture alone gives little evidence of a preferred layout.
Visual draft before clarification

No preference note was written.

Robot asked Do you prefer the base on your right and the cap on your left for future assemblies?

Person answered Yes, for my own assemblies, put the part I use first on my right and the next one on my left.

Written memory used for transfer

Assembly parts by order of use

For the person’s own assemblies, they asked for the part they use first on their right and the part they use next on their left.
