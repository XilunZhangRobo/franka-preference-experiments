# What the fixed-question robot learned from asking

These are the ten original isolated episodes. An audit question could change the written note before a fresh reader tried it. A question during a later task could also change the continuing robot’s tentative choice before execution. Those are different uses of clarification.

## Questions after the demonstration

9 of 10 episodes produced a question and a changed note. The remaining episode was material-order. Across matched fresh-reader comparisons, adding the spoken answer coincided with 3 incorrect-to-correct changes (gift-finishing, completion-update, lighting) and 1 correct-to-incorrect changes (vegetable-texture). One draw per condition leaves sampling variation. An unchanged binary choice can still hide a more precise note.

## Questions during a new task

The continuing robot asked on 7 of 20 later decisions. Of those, 6 tentative choices already matched and stayed correct, 1 changed to the correct action, 0 changed to an incorrect action, and 0 stayed incorrect. These observations are conditional on the robot choosing to ask.

## What to test next

The original results support testing whether the robot can defer a broad question until a concrete choice depends on the answer. Measure questions after demonstrations and during later choices separately, alongside transfer accuracy, scope-control accuracy, and corrections made after asking. The proposed one-in-five rate is a guide to interruption burden.
