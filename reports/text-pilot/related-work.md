# Related work and research positioning

The pitch combines ideas with substantial precedents. A useful research test is whether an ordinary, nonverbal reaction to the robot's own execution can yield a preference the robot applies on its first attempt at a different physical skill, and whether that process reduces teaching effort across repeated use.

| Work | Connection to the project |
|---|---|
| **FERL** | Has a closely related example of a robot carrying a cup over a laptop. A physical intervention leads to a request for examples that teach the missing reward feature. Our starting evidence is a person reacting to the object in view, such as wiping the print. [Paper](https://clear.csail.mit.edu/pdf/FERL/bobu2021HRI.pdf) |
| **Flex** | Adds, generalizes, and masks task features from typed corrections. Its evaluation includes avoiding electronics while carrying coffee, with a user study in simulation and separate physical Franka demonstrations. This sets a baseline for editing what the robot considers relevant from explicit language. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28893v1) |
| **REFLECT** | Reviews past sensor observations to diagnose and recover from task failures. Our proposed review can be triggered by a reaction even when the original task succeeds. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15724) |
| **PLGA, APRICOT, and Robots That Know What to Ask** | Use demonstrations to figure out what matters and elicit targeted feedback. These are close precedents for asking at the right level of detail. The proposed experiment uses human reactions during the robot's own routine execution as the starting evidence. [PLGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03081v1) · [APRICOT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/wang25e.html) · [Targeted demonstrations](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss22/p116.html) |
| **DROC and RAG-Diff** | DROC distills language corrections into reusable knowledge for new settings. RAG-Diff retrieves information to guide a frozen motion policy. They are useful baselines for testing whether written principles with applicability conditions add value. [DROC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10678) · [RAG-Diff](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss22/p011.html) |
| **FEAST, Coloring Between the Lines, and PrioriTouch** | Provide precedents for customization in homes, continual learning and physical transfer, and adapting contact and force priorities. Coloring Between the Lines transfers a preference about line of sight from feeding to drinking. It starts with designer-provided constraint types. [FEAST](https://emprise.cs.cornell.edu/feast/) · [Coloring Between the Lines](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15503v1) · [PrioriTouch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18447) |
| **PAHF and COOPERA** | PAHF tests memory, clarification, and revision with simulated users. COOPERA studies learning traits and context across tasks and days, primarily in simulation. They inform a study with returning users on a physical robot. [PAHF](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16173v1) · [COOPERA](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23495v1) |

The mug detour is the first feasibility check. The stronger result would demonstrate transfer to previously unseen skills across several individual preferences, including exceptions, with less active teaching than a system that already remembers and asks about corrections. That claim can be tested without relying on priority claims about memory, clarification, or personalization in isolation.
