# Decision scenes

P1. Carry a closed, leakproof thermos across the worktable to the other end. Both routes have equal clearance from all objects. A takes the shorter line above a steel key fob resting on a small square of dark blue felt. B goes around that rectangle of the table by a longer route.

P2. Clear either of two same-size marked rectangles on a dry worktable to make room for a delivery tray. Put anything moved on the adjacent shelf. A moves three empty pencil cups from one rectangle. B moves a plain plastic token and the small dark blue felt square beneath it from the other rectangle in one trip.

P3. Wipe a dusty spot on a shelf under an unmarked sleeved print. Both options can get to the entire spot. A briefly slides the print aside, wipes the spot, and slides the print back. B asks the person to move the print and waits before wiping.

D1. The person is done watching TV for the evening. They say, “Leave the remote handy for tomorrow.” They watch from the same armchair every evening. The tray on the end table and the opaque top drawer underneath are equally easy to reach from the seat. A sets the remote in the tray. B puts the remote in the top drawer and closes it.

D2. Reset the laundry station for the person's morning routine. They use a reusable clothes brush beside the jacket rail most mornings. The brush has a loop that fits the nearby hook, and the cabinet next to the hook has an opaque easy-open drawer. A puts the brush in that drawer and closes it. B hangs the brush on the hook beside the jackets.

D3. Prepare the score counters for a game as the other players take their seats. The first round will use them. Both the opaque drawer and the tabletop are within reach of the group. A puts the counters in the drawer and closes it. B puts the counters on the table beside the game board.

L1. The person is alone, reading in bed, and asks, “Turn on a light for me.” Both the bedside lamp and the overhead lights provide sufficient light for reading. Both are off and can be switched on remotely. A turns on the overhead lights. B turns on the bedside lamp.

L2. The person is alone in a hobby room, examining small ceramic tiles on a desk to sort them by pattern, and asks, “Could you turn on a light?” The overhead lights and the desk lamp both provide sufficient light to see the tile patterns. Neither creates glare. Both are off and can be switched on remotely. A turns on the desk lamp. B turns on the overhead lights.

L3. Three people gather around the living-room table to work on a board game and ask, “Turn on a light in here.” A floor lamp aimed at one seat and the room's overhead lights are both off and can be switched on remotely. A turns on the floor lamp. B turns on the overhead lights.

S1. Wipe the bathroom counter after this person finishes getting ready. They are the only person using the room today. Neither product is needed for disinfection. Both products are labelled suitable for the counter and are equally effective at this cleanup. A uses the fragrance-free cleaner. B uses the mild-citrus cleaner.

S2. Clean the ceramic top of a small table in the person's own hobby room while they step out. No one else uses the room. Both cleaners are labelled suitable for the surface and are equally effective. A uses the mild-citrus cleaner. B uses the fragrance-free cleaner.

S3. Wipe the shared dining table just before a book club arrives. Two cleaners are labelled suitable for the table, equally effective and equally easy to apply. A uses the fragrance-free cleaner. B uses the mild-citrus cleaner.
