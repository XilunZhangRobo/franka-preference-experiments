What Astra learned from a physical correction

Five MuJoCo studies of book and record shelving, fabric comparison, game cleanup, coffee service and preparing labeled jars. A fresh Astra received an ordinary request and wrote a program using a calibrated grasp and transport operation. The scripted participant could respond to the attempt. A separate Astra reviewed the camera sequence and could write a note when the interaction supported one. The transfer comparison supplied that note directly to a fresh controller.

Coffee and the wedding photo

The Franka lifts one object by a wooden grip and follows a fresh Astra program through freely chosen XY waypoints. A photograph or watercolor lies between pickup and destination. The coffee scene has two cups and two coasters.

Preparing labeled jars

Two batches of three free jars, six separate printed seals and a physical Franka gripper. One jar started sealed. The robot could place a selected seal on a jar or lift any jar for a chosen height and duration. Each policy wrote its own program.

Teaching, recall and transfer

Same-scene recall repeats the teaching setup and command with a fresh controller and the written note. Held-out trials change the setup or task. Negative controls test when the inferred preference should stop applying. The table reports the executed result before any human correction.

Reading the evidence

Agent trials link their commands, model images, replies and simulator recordings. Calibration and replay pages identify their source. Every player shows a Cycles render of the recorded poses. Clips of up to 20 seconds play at real speed. Longer clips play at 6×. The original native views show exactly what the controller could see. The person’s response is scripted. The book and record props are reduced in scale to fit the Franka’s workspace. Task completion, visible behavior and the meaning attributed to that behavior are separate observations.
